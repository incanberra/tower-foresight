I // NATIONAL INTELLIGENCE & INVESTMENT SECURITY

Commanding the Geoeconomic & Foreign Investment Frontline

Most recently, as Assistant Director-General leading the Economic Analysis Branch at the Office of National Intelligence (ONI) from 2020 to 2026, Shaun directed the Australian intelligence community’s peak economic assessment capability. Throughout the COVID-19 pandemic, the global inflation shock, and the sharp escalation of great-power geoeconomic rivalry, he delivered authoritative, contestable assessments to the Prime Minister and the National Security Committee of Cabinet. His branch’s work spanned economic coercion, critical mineral supply chains, energy transitions, sovereign debt vulnerabilities, and emerging financial technologies, while substantially expanding ONI’s direct engagement with senior Australian business leaders.

This intelligence leadership built directly upon his tenure as an SES Band 1 Principal Adviser in the Australian Treasury’s Foreign Investment Division (Complex Cases). Operating at the nexus of commercial capital and national security, Shaun managed Australia’s most sensitive and high-profile foreign investment applications—including landmark critical infrastructure proposals such as CKI/APA. Briefing the Foreign Investment Review Board (FIRB) weekly and advising the Treasurer, he negotiated directly with intelligence agencies, the Critical Infrastructure Centre, and the world’s largest sovereign and institutional investors (including Temasek and StateGrid) to craft pragmatic conditions that protected the national interest without chilling productive capital.

To deepen the empirical foundations of this work, Shaun completed a sabbatical year in the United Kingdom at the University of Nottingham’s School of Economics, graduating with an MSc in Economic Development and Policy Analysis with Distinction. His dissertation—which empirically modelled Strategic Factor Bias in China’s Outward Foreign Direct Investment—was awarded the prize for the best dissertation in the policy stream, before he returned to Treasury to help establish the analytical architecture of the newly formed Centre for Population.

II // INDO-PACIFIC DIPLOMACY & SOVEREIGN REFORM

Six Years on the Ground in ASEAN and the Pacific

Unlike advisers whose regional perspective is formed solely from Canberra or global financial capitals, Shaun spent six years posted overseas representing the Australian Government in Southeast Asia and the Pacific. As Treasury’s Senior Representative to South-East Asia based at the Australian Embassy in Jakarta (2015–2018), he led regional economic coverage across ASEAN and served as Australia’s APEC Senior Finance Official during the Philippines and Vietnam host years. Travelling extensively across the region, he cultivated an exceptional network—from semiconductor fabricators in Penang and sovereign asset managers in Singapore to central bankers in Jakarta and Communist Party economists in Hanoi—translating regional political-economy shifts into strategic reporting for Canberra.

Earlier, during a three-year deployment to Honiara with the Regional Assistance Mission to Solomon Islands (RAMSI), Shaun led the Economic Reform Unit embedded inside the Solomon Islands Ministry of Finance and Treasury. Confronting the immediate shock of the 2008 Global Financial Crisis, he identified looming foreign-exchange exhaustion, rallied international donor coordination, and helped negotiate and implement an IMF Standby Credit Facility. Over three years, he spearheaded the deregulation of the country’s telecommunications and international aviation markets, drove State-Owned Enterprise privatisations, and transitioned the unit from expatriate dependency to a locally led economic policy division.

Between these overseas postings, Shaun held senior leadership roles in Canberra managing Australia’s international financial engagement—serving as Manager of the G20 Policy Engagement and Strategy Team during Australia’s landmark 2014 G20 Presidency (coordinating the Cairns and Washington Finance Ministers’ meetings and supporting the Brisbane Leaders’ Summit) and managing Australia’s relationships with the Asian Development Bank and African Development Bank, including stints as Acting SES Band 1 General Manager of Treasury’s International Finance and Development Division.

III // REGULATORY ARCHITECTURE, POLITICS & THE PRESS GALLERY

Financial Stability, Ministerial Strategy, and Executive Clarity

Shaun’s grasp of domestic regulatory mechanics was forged during the Global Financial Crisis in Treasury’s Financial System Division, where he worked alongside the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA), APRA, and the Council of Financial Regulators to design and legislate Australia’s Financial Claims Scheme—taking the banking and general insurance guarantee from high-level principle to Cabinet approval, industry negotiation, and statutory drafting, while also representing Australia on financial services chapters in free trade negotiations with China, ASEAN, and the United States.

Crucially for corporate boards and government leaders alike, Shaun combines technical economic mastery with rare political and communications fluency. Before joining Treasury, he spent four years in Federal Parliament as a Senior Political, Parliamentary, and Prudential Adviser to the Minister for Communications, IT and the Arts and the Minister for Revenue and Assistant Treasurer—steering the parliamentary passage of the Telstra sale legislation, media ownership reforms, medical indemnity tort law reforms, and Cabinet tactics. His career began with six years as a newspaper journalist at The West Australian, culminating in two years in the Canberra Federal Parliamentary Press Gallery covering the 1998 Federal Election and the 1999 and 2000 Commonwealth Budgets.
